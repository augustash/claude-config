What the law asks of {{ENTITY}}

The ADA’s rule for public agencies requires {{ENTITY}}’s websites to meet WCAG 2.1, Level AA: 50 criteria, such as “every image has a text alternative” and “everything works from a keyboard”. There is no passing score. Each criterion is met or it isn’t, on every page, and a task that takes several steps counts only if every step does.

How we weigh what we find

The rule treats a shortfall as acceptable when it barely matters: when people with disabilities can still get the same information and do the same things, as quickly, privately and independently as anyone else. So we rank problems by how much they stop people, not by how many there are, and recommend the ones that block someone first.

How we report

We test pages on desktop and phone, with automated checks, by keyboard and with a screen reader. Each of the 50 criteria is then marked Supports, Partially supports, Does not support, Not applicable or Not yet tested. We never mark a criterion as met without testing it. Each recommendation names the criteria it meets or moves toward.

This is a record of what we tested and found, not legal advice or a certification. Which deadline applies to {{ENTITY}} is a question for its counsel.
